User guide

Sequencer

Six independent 32-step sequences, polyrhythm, length, volume, copy/paste and playhead.

Six sequences, six clocks

Metulifer contains 6 sequences (numSequences = 6) displayed as rows. Each sequence owns its own step counter: they are not locked to one another. That is what makes polyrhythm possible: a 7-step sequence can loop while another runs 16 steps.

  • One step = a sixteenth note (1/16).
  • Step duration: sampleRate × 60 / (BPM × 4). At 120 BPM and 44.1 kHz: 5,512.5 samples (≈ 125 ms).
  • Tempo is global (tempo, 20–300 BPM).

Steps & Click-and-Drag Paintbrush

Each row holds 32 step buttons:

  • Single Click: toggles step state (active = amber backlight).
  • Click-and-Drag Paintbrush: hold the mouse button and sweep across the row horizontally to paint or erase a continuous chain of steps in one smooth motion.
    • If the initial step clicked was off, dragging activates every hovered step.
    • If the initial step clicked was on (or via right-click), dragging erases steps.
    • Continuous interpolation ensures no steps are missed even during very fast mouse sweeps.
  • Downbeat accents (steps 1, 5, 9, 13…) feature distinct slate styling while inactive for clear 4/4 grouping.
  • The pattern is stored as a 32-bit bitfield per sequence, inside a ValueTree serialised with the plugin state — not in host parameters.

Why not parameters?

Storing 192 steps as automatable parameters would pollute the DAW automation lane. The pattern lives in stepsTree (properties p0…p5) and is saved by getStateInformation().

Length and volume

ControlParameterRangeDefault
LengthseqN_length1–32 steps32
VolumeseqN_volume0–100 %80 %

Length sets the sequence's loop period: past it, the counter returns to step 0. Reducing the length to 7 turns a 32-step sequence into a 7-step loop (steps 8 to 32 are ignored).

Length and volume are per sequence

These two knobs are permanent: they stay attached to their row, unlike synth knobs which follow the selection.

Korg Electribe EMX-1 & ESX-1 Inspired Mute / Solo Workflow

Metulifer's mute architecture emulates classic hardware groovebox behavior:

  1. Non-destructive Mute (Tail Preservation): Clicking [M] does not truncate audio rendering. The DSP engine simply ceases generating new gateOn step triggers. Active notes release smoothly, and stereo delay / reverb decays naturally.
  2. Solo Memory: Engaging one or multiple [S] buttons isolates the soloed tracks. Disengaging solo instantly recovers the prior mute configuration without user intervention.
  3. DAW Automation: Automate every track's mute and solo state through host parameters seq1_mute … seq6_mute and seq1_solo … seq6_solo.

The row-level ARP button allows you to engage melodic variations on the fly during live performance, with detailed chord and algorithm settings configured in the Arpeggiator panel.

Playhead

During playback, an orange marker moves along each row, at that sequence's current step. The marker is refreshed at 60 Hz with zero-latency synchronization by the editor timer (not by the audio thread).

When stopped, markers disappear and counters restart from zero on the next Play.

Selection and pagination

Clicking a row selects it (blue accent bar on the left) and:

  • re-binds the synth, VCF, reverb and LFO cables to that sequence;
  • updates the display (SEQ n);
  • filters the visible modulation cables.

Selection is purely visual: it does not change how the other sequences sound.

DAW Host Synchronization (Host Sync)

Metulifer provides two complementary clock modes:

  1. Internal Clock (Free Run): The transport engine is controlled via the plugin's Play/Stop button and tempo rotary knob (20 to 300 BPM).
  2. Host Synchronization (Host Sync): When the Sync button in the Clock panel is engaged, the sequencer slave-locks completely to the host Digital Audio Workstation (DAW):
    • Automatic Start & Stop: The transport starts and halts in direct synchrony with the DAW timeline playback.
    • Continuous Tempo Tracking: Any dynamic tempo changes or automation curves on the DAW tempo track are tracked sample-accurately.
    • Phase Locking (PPQ) : The playhead synchronizes to absolute musical time (ppqPosition) on sixteenth-note (1/16) boundaries, ensuring that every step aligns perfectly with the DAW project grid even after scrubbing or jumping around the timeline.

Creative Algorithmic Tools

In addition to manual step toggling and the Drag-and-Paintbrush mode, the sequencer includes three algorithmic pattern engines per track:

1. Circular Step Shift

Rotates the active steps of a track to the left or to the right while preserving the sequence's loop length.

  • Shifting right causes steps that exit the end of the active loop to re-emerge at step 0 (circular rotation modulo active loop length).
  • Perfect for creating syncopated variations and shifting the rhythmic pocket against other sequences.

2. Euclidean Distribution (Bjorklund's Algorithm)

Generates a mathematically optimal distribution of k pulses across n steps as evenly as possible.

  • Based on Bjorklund's particle accelerator timing algorithm and found throughout traditional world rhythms (bossa nova, tresillo, rumba, Afro-Cuban bell patterns).
  • Instantly turns any row into an hypnotic polyrhythmic groove.

3. Density Randomizer

Generates a stochastic pattern based on a target density percentage (0% = all steps off, 100% = all steps active).

  • Ideal for fast rhythmic brainstorming and dynamic percussion synthesis.

Polyphonic MIDI Output (MIDI Out)

Every sequence in Metulifer can transmit sample-accurate MIDI events to external destinations:

  • Active steps trigger sample-accurate noteOn and noteOff events routed to the plugin's MIDI output bus.
  • Dedicated multi-channel routing:
    • Sequence 1 ➔ MIDI Channel 1
    • Sequence 2 ➔ MIDI Channel 2
    • Sequence 3 ➔ MIDI Channel 3
    • Sequence 4 ➔ MIDI Channel 4
    • Sequence 5 ➔ MIDI Channel 5
    • Sequence 6 ➔ MIDI Channel 6
  • Allows capturing the sequencer patterns directly into the DAW piano roll or driving external hardware synthesizers and software instruments.
  • When playback stops, an allNotesOff message is dispatched across all 16 MIDI channels to eliminate hanging notes.

Internal wiring

The audio thread never reads the ValueTree: it reads an array of atomic ints (patterns[6]) mirrored by a listener on the message thread.
